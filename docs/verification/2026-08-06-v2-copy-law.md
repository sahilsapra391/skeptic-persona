## 2. Embedded attribution, and the hole the first cut opened

Attribution is satisfied when the publishing source is the named actor of the
fact line. `[6]` ("A company just told the SEC...") and `[7]` ("The FTC just
sued...") install on that basis with no trailing phrase at all.

`structuralCheck` needed the same amendment: it demanded `/\bper /` on the
first segment and would have rejected both exemplars after `checkRegister`
accepted them. Both now resolve through the same `attributionVerdict`, so the
two gates cannot disagree about one sentence.

**The hole, caught by the CFTC cross-authority kill-test.** The first cut let
embedded attribution satisfy the rule and stopped looking. So:

```
"CFTC orders a $35,000 payment, per SEC."   ->  PASSED
```

The fact line names CFTC as its own actor, attribution was satisfied, and the
wrong agency rode along unchecked. A cross-source citation is a fabricated
provenance claim; it is the class this repo has closed twice. Fixed: a
registered citation that is not this item's source **always** fails, embedded
attribution or not. Longest-match resolution, so `per SEC Form 4` is not read
as also carrying a bare `per SEC`.

## 3. Redundancy, and what it changed in live rendering

`"FTC sued, per FTC"` is flagged (`attribution_redundant`). Detection is
deliberately narrow in two ways, and both narrowings came from live data.

**The verb must be lowercase.** `"SEC: SEC Charges Two Individuals, per SEC"`
is the shape `reg.headline` builds from a feed title. It states the source
three times, but that is D-15, a rendering defect, and hard-failing it would
have refused live press drafts wholesale.

**Only the head line's first sentence counts.** Found in FDA recall #982:

```
Modern Warrior Life, LLC is recalling product. Class I, reason: FDA analysis
revealed the presence of unapproved ingredients...
```

The firm acts; the FDA appears only inside the quoted reason. Scanning the
whole line dropped `per FDA` from a post that genuinely needed it — source and
actor DO differ there. First cut got this wrong; the test pins it.

## 4. The aphorism rule

`aphorismCheck` in `src/rag/validate.ts`, backed by
`src/rag/definitions.ts`. Extract the definitional claim, try to bind it to
the payload or the registry, flag what stays unbound.

Binding runs through the registry by design, because that is where the
owner's own split lives:

- **rule** entries are true whatever we parsed, so they bind with no payload.
- **record** entries cash out to a count, so they bind only when the payload
  carries it. "Two is a pattern" passes with a repeat count of 2 in the
  payload and **fails without it** — pinned in both directions.

Widening what may be said means writing a definition with a citation. That is
the point, not a side effect.

### The owner's named cases

```
NEG  "The filing is the announcement."   flagged
NEG  "A 144 is the intent."              flagged (and still flagged against a real Form 144 payload)
POS  "The buy is the only statement insiders are allowed to make."   passes, bound to Regulation FD
POS  "One is a mistake. Two is a pattern."                           passes, bound to the record count
```

**Detector bug found and fixed before shipping.** The first cut consumed the
sentence terminator as its left boundary, so in "A grant is a paycheck. A P is
a decision." only the first claim was ever extracted. Two definitional lines
back to back is the exact shape the rule exists to judge. Now sentence-at-a-time.

### What it finds in artefacts that teach the model — D-31

The scorer runs on generated output. It is **not** in `FLOOR_GATES`, because
running it there would fail the owner's own signed beat library, and that text
is his.

`test/aphorism.test.ts` pins the full list instead of hiding it. **22 distinct
unbound definitional lines** live in the beat library and exemplar bank. The
one that matters:

> **"A 144 is the intent. The Form 4 is the receipt."** is one of the two
> lines the owner named as RETIRED. It is not retired. It is signed at
> `docs/persona.md:112`, live at `archetypes.ts:240`, and rendered into
> **11 pending cards** right now.

The rest split into two groups. Metaphors ("A grant is a paycheck", "The item
number is the confession", "The cluster is the fact", "A hold is a decision
made by people who seriously considered moving") and provenance statements
("The stake number is the filer's own", "The class is the FDA's grading, not
ours", "The weekly change is the CFTC's own figure"). The provenance group
arguably DOES cash out — to the payload's own attribution — and one registry
entry would bind all of them. Writing that entry is a voice call on signed
text, so it goes to the owner rather than being decided here.

**A correction the fetch produced.** The carried-forward note said "Reg SHO
five-day". There is no five-day close-out. The five consecutive settlement
days (with 10,000 shares and 0.5% of shares outstanding) define a **threshold
security**; mandatory close-out runs on **thirteen** consecutive settlement
days. The registry entry states both and a test asserts it never claims
otherwise — a fabricated rule wearing a real citation is worse than no entry.
