# p5-12 verification: Senate eFD arrival latency

**Measured:** 2026-08-06 UTC against the live `skeptic-wire` D1 database.

The plan: *"Senate eFD arrival-latency measurement forward (already framed as
intermittent, now measure it): weekly latency line in the digest."*

## 1. What the measurement found

```
SELECT COUNT(*), MIN(julianday(fetched_at)-julianday(event_at)),
                 MAX(julianday(fetched_at)-julianday(event_at))
FROM items WHERE source='senate_ptr' AND event_at IS NOT NULL;
-> 4 filings | fastest 4.56 days | slowest 7.56 days

source_state.senate_ptr: total_failures 3 | consecutive_failures 3
                         last_ok_at 2026-08-02T13:30:01Z
```

**Senate PTR filings reach us between 4.6 and 7.6 days after the senator
files.** And the source has not polled successfully since 2026-08-02.

Both numbers matter more than they look, because CONGRESS_PTR is the flagship:
base 90, the highest in the salience table, ceiling-exempt by owner amendment,
and given a 96-hour TTL precisely because it is the lane worth waiting for. A
filing that arrives 7.6 days after it was filed has already spent more than its
own TTL in transit before we ever see it.

## 2. Why the line carries TWO numbers

Latency alone cannot distinguish **eFD publishing late** from **us polling
badly**, and the two imply opposite fixes: a freshness allowance versus a
retry. So the digest line prints the poll-failure counters beside the latency,
always, including on the no-filings branch.

That is migration 0059's lesson applied rather than restated. Its comment:

> a source that fails two polls in three leaves NO trace: a week of one-in-three
> landing looks identical in D1 to a week of perfect polls [...] "were our polls
> failing, or does eFD index late" could not be answered retroactively at all.

`last_ok_at` is overwritten by every success and `consecutive_failures` resets,
so the evidence destroys itself. This chunk reports from `items` rows, which
are never overwritten, so next week the question is still answerable.

## 3. What the digest will print

```
Senate eFD arrival latency, last 7 days:
  N filing(s): median X days from filing to ingest, slowest Y
  Small sample: N filing(s). A direction, not a measurement.
  Polling: 3 consecutive failure(s) now, 3 lifetime, last success 2026-08-02 13:30Z
  Latency above includes our polling gap, not eFD's publishing lag alone.
```

With four filings lifetime, the seven-day window is thin by construction. The
line says so rather than presenting a median of one or two as a finding, on the
same rule the zero-edit rate already follows.

## 4. The branch that matters most is the empty one

**No filings is not zero latency.** A quiet week and a lane that has stopped
arriving produce an identical filing count, and only the poll counters separate
them. So on the no-filings branch the digest prints:

```
  No filings arrived in the window, so there is no latency to report.
  Polling: 3 consecutive failure(s) now, ...
```

Never `0.0 days`. Pinned by a test in both directions.

## 5. What this does NOT do, stated plainly

It does not fix the eFD 503s. `senate_ptr` remains intermittent (the registry
entry stands), and this chunk is the measurement the plan asked for, not a
retry policy. What it changes is that the intermittency now leaves a weekly
trace instead of being re-litigated from memory each time someone notices.

## 6. Tests

Six added:
- median and slowest render against a stated filing count
- **no filings renders no latency and still prints the poll counters**
- a sample under five is labelled a direction, not a measurement
- the polling-gap confound is named rather than implied away
- never-succeeded is said outright, not rendered as a blank timestamp
- latency is computed from real `items` rows, filed-to-ingest, in days

Suite: **1,056 passing**, 1 pre-existing failure (D-6).
